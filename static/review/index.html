A systematic review, without the overhead.

A systematic review gathers every study on a question with a planned search, then decides in the open which ones count, so others can check and repeat it. The steps here follow that order. Import your search results, screen titles and abstracts with one key per decision, check full texts, extract data into a form you control, and get a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram that fills itself in.

Drop search exports here

RIS, BibTeX (.bib), CSV (Scopus, Publish or Perish, and others), Web of Science plain text, or PubMed (.nbib / .txt). RIS and BibTeX are file formats that databases and reference managers export.

How do I get a search export?
  1. Run your search in the database and check the number of results.
  2. Find the export option. In PubMed it is Send to, then Citation manager (an .nbib file). In Web of Science it is Export, then RIS. In Scopus it is Export, then RIS or CSV.
  3. Export every result, with abstracts. Some databases export in batches, for example 1,000 at a time, so save each batch.
  4. Drop the files here, one search at a time. Each database becomes a source on your PRISMA diagram.

Sources

Duplicates

Deduplicating means setting aside copies of the same paper found in more than one search. A DOI is the permanent ID printed on most papers (it starts with 10.). A record counts as a duplicate when its DOI matches, or its title matches and the years agree. The earliest imported copy is kept. If a record is not really a copy, press Not a duplicate to put it back into screening.

Type words from your inclusion and exclusion criteria, separated by commas. They are coloured in every title and abstract. Highlighting only helps you read. It never makes a decision.

PRISMA 2020 flow diagram

PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) is the standard flow diagram that reports how records moved through a review: how many were found, set aside as duplicates, screened, excluded and why, and finally included. This one is built from your sources and decisions, and it updates as you screen.

Records marked "Maybe" at title and abstract screening move on to full-text review. Each included study is counted as one report. Check the counts against your own records before publishing.
