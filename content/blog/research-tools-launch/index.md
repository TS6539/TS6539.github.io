Three tools I built for my own research work are now live on this site. Each one runs entirely in your browser, and each one is free to use.

<a href="/coder/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Transcript Coder</a> (version 0.4.1, pre-alpha) is for coding interview transcripts one sentence at a time. It offers a guided path for first-time coders and an expert path if you already know your method, and it exports your work for thematic analysis or epistemic network analysis (ENA). It works on computers and phones, and your transcripts stay in your browser.

<a href="/review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Review Kit</a> (version 0.1.1, pre-alpha) supports systematic reviews. Import your search results, screen titles and abstracts and then full texts, extract data into a form you design, and get a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram that fills itself in from your decisions. Everything exports to Excel, and everything stays in your browser.

<a href="/peer-review/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Peer Review Desk</a> (version 0.2) walks you through a journal manuscript review in nine steps, from the first read to the final checks, and exports a Word document to send to the authors. Confidential comments for the editor are kept separate and are never included in the author copy.

All three are early versions, so if you try one and something breaks, please tell me.
